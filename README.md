## Curiosity

I never started programming because I wanted a career in technology.

I started because computers fascinated me.

Every new concept felt like discovering another level in a game. What began with exploring computers eventually led me to HTML, where for the first time I could build something real—something people could actually see and interact with.

That experience shaped how I think about software even today.

I've always believed that great software isn't only about writing code. It's about creating experiences that make technology accessible to everyone. Most people won't understand the architecture behind an application, but they immediately understand how it feels to use it. That's why user experience has always remained close to me, no matter where my journey has taken me.

Curiosity has quietly guided every step since then.

It took me from frontend development to backend engineering, from backend systems to Linux, from Linux to AI infrastructure, and into building software that spans multiple layers of a system.

I don't chase technologies.

I follow curiosity.

---

## Engineering Philosophy

These are a few principles that guide how I approach software.

- Understand the system before optimizing it.
- Curiosity creates better engineers than comfort.
- Build solutions that are simple, reliable, and maintainable.
- Learn concepts deeply instead of memorizing tools.
- Technology changes; good engineering principles don't.

## Beyond Engineering

One thing I've learned is that I rarely enjoy staying on the surface of anything.

Whether it's a framework, an operating system, AI infrastructure, or a deployment pipeline, I naturally want to understand how it works beneath the abstraction.

That curiosity has shaped the way I learn, the way I build, and the kind of engineer I'm becoming.
